# Irish in Europe after the derogation

Working source note for a substantial long-form article scheduled for
publication on **1 January 2027**, the fifth anniversary of the formal end of
the Irish-language derogation. This is research material, not a published site
project. It must not be linked from the generated site before publication.

The EU publications in
`public/documents/european-union/` are third-party primary and reference
sources; they are not authored by Éimí.

## Research frame

The formal Irish-language derogation ended on 1 January 2022. The article is
concerned with the gap between that formal equality and the practical
experience of finding timely, complete, and discoverable EU information in
Irish.

The official baseline is clear:

- The European Commission described Irish as being on an equal footing with
  the other 23 official EU languages and said the institutions were ready to
  meet demand for Irish translation.
- The Irish Government said the end of the derogation meant that every
  document published by the EU would now be translated into Irish.
- The Commission's June 2021 report concluded that the institutions had
  sufficient capacity to apply Regulation No 1 without an Irish-language
  derogation from 1 January 2022.

Those commitments are the baseline to test, not a conclusion that practical
parity has already been achieved.

## Local comparative reference set

The local collection includes Gaeilge and English editions of materials about
the European Parliament, the European Council and Council of the EU, a short
guide to the EU, the European Democracy Passport, and the EU colouring book.
The Charter of Fundamental Rights is present in Gaeilge, English, and German.
These are comparison material for research and drafting, not portfolio
outputs.

The two colouring-book PDFs exceed Cloudflare Workers' 25 MiB per-file limit.
They remain in the repository but are excluded from deployment through
